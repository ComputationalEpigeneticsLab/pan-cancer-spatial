# Multiplex spatial profiling elucidates tumor microenvironmental heterogeneity and potential therapeutic vulnerabilities
Weiwei Zhou1,2#, Minghai Su1,#, Qingyi Yang2,#, Yuxin Wan1#, Jiaxu E1, Meijun liu1, Si Li2, Kang Xu2, Tiantongfei Jiang1*, Juan Xu1,3*, Yongsheng Li2,3*

## Abstract
Spatial transcriptome reveals valuable insights into cellular heterogeneity within tumor microenvironments (TMEs), paving a way to deeply understand the cellular mechanisms contributing to cancer. Here, we construct a pan-cancer spatial transcriptomic atlas, including 2,640,790 spots across 787 tissue slices in 35 solid tumors. We delineate 17 malignant meta-programs that converge into six spatial ecosystems reflecting immune states. Notably, tumor boundary neighborhoods exhibit immunosuppressive features, where cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) and tumor-associated macrophages co-localize to form tumor immune barrier and impede cytotoxic immune infiltration and sustain tumor stemness. Tumor immune barrier is correlated with immune checkpoint blockade resistance and spatially resolved ligand-receptor interactions implicate CAF–tumor communication in therapy resistance. We propose SMART (Spatial Microenvironment-guided Assessment of Risk in Tumors) for identification of diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers in cancer. Overall, we portray a pan-cancer spatial transcriptome atlas and illuminate the spatial heterogeneity of cancer, laying a foundation for identification of biomarkers.
